Theres no longer any doubt in my mind that the pace at which the world is changing is accelerating. 

“There are decades where nothing happens; and there are weeks where decades happen” 

 *-wrongly attributed to Lenin* 

Within the past months alone, math problems that have stumped humans for decades have been solved. Newer AI models are being released on what seems like an accelerating pace. Cyber outages and incidents pop up like acne for a teenager. Talks of AGI, RSI have all grown louder. When last year around this time, I hopped on Claude code for the first time and felt like I was so ahead of the bell curve, now, agentic coding tools have become commonplace.

Amidst all this change, is the my personal realization that nothing remains sacred. For a while, math was used as an example of something that AI could never do. I remember in 2023, one common example of AI’s shortcomings was asking it basic math problems and seeing how it spectacularly failed. Yet in the 3 years since then, AI models have become better at math than humans are. Similarly, coding was one of the things that originally people denied was doable by AI. The code quality trash. AI doesn’t understand code style. But the models continued to get better. We can move the goalpost and say that the current thing that AI can’t do is do proper system design, but how long before the models incorporate that. How long before software becomes an entire dark factory?

I do think beyond that though, I question what does it mean to be human. Its easy to hold onto shreds of identity and defend those stakes in the sand as innately human. That the present models are incapable of doing XYZ thing that I can. But I think I’m convinced that nothing remains innately sacred anymore. Not system design, not soft skills, not design taste. Ultimately, I can’t point to any 1 individual trait that remains unreplaceable.

I want to turn to the past as a form of comfort. Throughout history, Silicon Valley has had no shortages of “world ending” innovations. I wonder to what extent is this belief in the sheer magnitude of change a factor that we’re about to witness a figment of my youth, and how much of it is due to tangible change that is meaningfully different. The historical part of my brain wants to say that the truth ends up being somewhere between the fearful and the greedy. The rational part of my brain  quips back that there’s never been a point in history where raw intelligence is the medium of innovation.

Part of me wishes I could pause the world for a couple of months. Let me live out the remainder of my college years and to enjoy being 20, young and dumb with friends in college. I want to laugh too loud, stay out too long, have conversations that feel like they have no end while I still can with the people who matter to me. I want to enjoy this era of my life wholeheartedly. I wish I could pause the world for a moment and just let me enjoy that. But I know that can’t happen.

So where does that leave me? I really don’t know. I have no clue what the future will hold, even as I remain naively optimistic that we as humanity will come to the correct decisions, I don’t know what that end state is. I suppose questions about what am I, what is my identity in this post AGI world will have to remain unanswered for the meantime. 

What I do know is that as an individual, I have drunken the koolaid enough to where I don’t think I could bury my head in the sand and still live a content life. The world is changing fast and I want to be a part of it and witness that change firsthand. I’m pretty sure I would be naively happier if I could ignore what was going on and focus on being in school and enjoying this era of my life wholeheartedly. 

But I can’t.

Call it ambition, call it hubris, call it being a slave to a self conceived ideal. I don’t think I can be whole heartedly content sitting on the sidelines as decades happen in weeks. I want to see what is out there, to be a part of the change, to go through struggles and successes. At least for now, I don’t think anything else would answer those questions for me other than to go try for myself.

I also remain naively excited for the future. Maybe that’s a nice thing about not being super tapped into research. I think there’s still plenty of opportunity out there that I want to be a part of. I think it’s such an exciting time to be alive with all that’s out there and the new frontier that we’ve just begun to discover. Whether this frontier is the last, I’ll get back to you in a couple months.

## Other Considerations

To be fair, I’ve been thinking about taking a gap semester for a while now. Every since the start of the summer really, I was thinking about the possibility. A lot of my friends are going to be studying abroad, which makes the social opportunity cost a lot lower to gap, which is nice. I’m sure I’ll have friends no matter where I end up, but I still think that there’s less of a losing the college experience component junior winter.

Also I think a big component of the choice to work is that I’ve been pretty interested in startups for a while, but I’m not fully convinced whether I want to go down the startups or quant path. I want to give both a shot and make an informed decision as to what’s the *best* for me. And with the prospect of having to intern junior summer, taking a gap semester junior winter to work at a startup makes the most sense in those timelines. It also will help expose me to some of the more current problems that people are working on. Especially if I want to found down the line, this is an important stepping stone towards that.

My hope is that after this gap semester, I’ve been able to answer if startups is the right path for me. Hopefully the startup that I join is one that I’d want to stay at, but even if it isn’t, I still think I could tell if I want to go to startups full time or remain on the quant path. 

I also think that gapping would be really fun. I’ve spent 2.5 years at Ann Arbor for school. I think throughout that time I’ve had a lot of great experiences at college. But, the prospect of living on my own, of experience actual adulthood seems really fun.

I also think that having spent a while in college, I’ve exhausted a lot of the learnings and growth I could have here. Don’t get me wrong, I still think I would learn in school, but I think I would comparatively learn lightyears more in the real world. Especially in the ways that I want to.
